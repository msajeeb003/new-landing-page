n8n vs Zapier vs Make.com for AI lead qualification

How n8n, Zapier and Make.com compare for one AI lead-qualification workflow: what each one bills for, the AI step, custom code, hosting and when to pick each.

A new lead fills in a form. Someone has to read it, decide whether it's a good fit, put it in the CRM and make sure the right person follows up. That's an ideal job for an automation with one AI step in the middle, and n8n, Zapier and Make.com can all run it. They differ in how they charge, how much control you get over the AI step, and who on your team can maintain the result.

I build automations in all three for remote teams, including at Artech Digital, so this comparison is about one concrete workflow rather than a feature checklist.

The workflow we're comparing

A typical AI lead-qualification flow looks like this. It's the shape of the flow in my operations automation case study.

  1. Trigger: a webhook receives a new lead from a form or landing page.
  2. Store: the lead is created in the CRM, for example HubSpot.
  3. Qualify: an AI model (Claude, in my case study) reads the lead against your criteria and returns a score and a short reason.
  4. Route: good-fit leads become a sales task (for example in Trello) with a follow-up; the rest go to a nurture list.
  5. Notify: the team gets a message, and a person only steps in where a decision is needed.

How the cost adds up per lead

The flow above has five or six steps. That matters more than the price list.

  • On Zapier and Make.com, every action step in the run is billed: a task on Zapier, a credit on Make. The AI step can cost more than a normal step. Ten leads a day is cheap on any of them; thousands of leads a month with extra enrichment steps add up quickly.
  • On n8n's paid plans, the whole run is one execution, no matter how many steps it has. n8n describes an execution as "a single run of your entire workflow".
  • Self-hosted n8n (the Community edition) is free to run. You pay for the server and for your AI model's tokens, and you look after updates and uptime yourself.

When to pick each

Pick n8n when:

  • lead volume is high or the flow has many steps (enrichment, scoring, several routes);
  • you want to keep lead data on your own server;
  • the qualification step needs more than one prompt, such as an AI agent that looks things up with tools before it scores (n8n's AI Agent node connects a model, memory and tools);
  • someone on the team is comfortable with a slightly more technical tool.

Pick Zapier when:

  • the people who'll maintain the automation aren't technical;
  • you depend on a niche app that only has a ready-made Zapier connector;
  • volume is modest, so per-task billing stays small.

Pick Make.com when:

  • you want to see every branch of the flow visually, with routers and filters;
  • the flow has several paths but moderate volume;
  • your team already builds scenarios in Make.

If your team already pays for one of the three and knows it well, that's usually the right choice. Rebuilding a working flow on another platform is rarely worth it unless volume or data rules force the move.

What makes AI lead scoring reliable, on any platform

The tool matters less than how the AI step is set up.

  1. Give the model a fixed rubric. Spell out what a good-fit lead looks like (industry, company size, budget, timeline) instead of asking "is this a good lead?".
  2. Ask for structured output. Request a score, a fit label and a one-line reason in a fixed JSON format, so the next step can route on it.
  3. Check the output before routing. If the response is missing a field or isn't valid JSON, send the lead to a person instead of guessing.
  4. Keep a person in the loop for borderline scores. Auto-route the clear yes and the clear no; review the middle.
  5. Log every decision. Store the score and the reason in the CRM, so you can see why a lead was routed and improve the rubric over time.
  6. Test on real leads before launch. Run last month's leads through the flow and compare the AI's calls with what your sales team actually decided.
